Wall Water Damage Repair in Calabasas, CA

Repair starts after the wall is dry, not before. This is the order of work, the materials involved, and the checks that older homes need first.

Call (818) 337-2642

By the Calabasas Water Damage Pros team · Updated October 28, 2026

Repair comes after drying, in a set order

A wall that looks fixed but still reads wet inside will stain through new paint and may grow mold behind it. So the rebuild waits until moisture meter readings in the framing and the surrounding drywall match a dry reference point in the same house. That can take several days depending on how wet the cavity was.

Once readings are normal, the sequence is predictable: remove what cannot be saved, check the framing, insulate, close the wall, finish the surface, then paint. Each step affects the next. A good scope lists them in that order with the materials named, and the price is walked through with you before work begins.

If you are still at the stage of stopping the spread, that belongs to water mitigation. The work below picks up after it.

Drywall or plaster: what you actually have

Most newer walls are gypsum board, sheets of gypsum between paper faces, nailed or screwed to studs. Wet drywall that has sagged, stained through, or stayed damp for a day or two is usually cut out and replaced. Patching it back to a flat, even surface is routine work: new board, tape, joint compound in coats, sanding, primer.

Plaster is built differently. It is applied over wood lath or a metal mesh, and it is heavy and hard. Plaster that is still firm and bonded can sometimes dry in place and be repaired with patching compound. If it has gone soft, pulled off the lath, or crumbles, sections come out and are rebuilt, either in plaster or with board.

A crew can tell you which you have by tapping and by looking at the thickness at an outlet opening. If you are unsure, ask before the demolition starts, because a plaster wall changes both the method and the schedule.

Insulation, baseboards and what goes back

Wet batt insulation loses its value as it slumps and holds water against the studs. It is removed from the affected bays and replaced once the framing is dry. Spray foam and rigid foam behave differently and may only need drying, so the type matters.

Baseboards come off during drying so air can reach the bottom of the wall cavity. Solid wood trim can often be saved and reinstalled. Trim made of pressed fiberboard swells and breaks, so it is normally replaced. Door casings, cabinet toe kicks and outlet covers get the same check.

Bottom plates and studs are inspected with a meter and a screwdriver. Sound, dry framing stays. Soft or split framing needs a licensed carpenter or contractor.

Painting and finish, and why stains return

Primer comes first. A stain-blocking primer seals old water marks, but only on a wall that is dry. After that, a finish coat has to match the sheen and the texture, and that is harder than it sounds. Matching a knockdown or orange-peel texture on a patch is usually what separates a hidden repair from a visible one.

Painting the full wall from corner to corner, instead of the patch, makes color differences disappear. Plan on that if the repair is large or the old paint has faded.

Older homes: test before you cut or sand

Before demolition or sanding in an older house, two hazards deserve a check. The Contractors State License Board flags 1945 to 1978 as the span when a build or remodel may have brought in asbestos material. Joint compound, textured ceilings, floor tile and some insulation are common places. A lab test of a small sample answers the question before dust is made.

For lead, the EPA's renovation, repair and painting rule applies to paid jobs that disturb paint in pre-1978 housing. Firms doing that work must follow lead-safe practices and be approved under the program, so ask to see it.

Asbestos abatement has its own rules. The CSLB notes it is illegal to advertise asbestos removal unless you hold the state's credential, and Cal/OSHA registers abatement contractors. If a test comes back positive, a general restoration crew should stop and hand that part to a registered abatement company.

Ask before work begins

Has this wall been tested if the house predates 1978? Who is the licensed contractor on the job? Is the scope in writing? A reliable answer to all three is the sign of a company worth hiring.

What drives repair cost, and how to compare bids

No honest number can come from a phone description. These are the real variables:

  • How much wall was wet, by height and by length
  • Whether the cavity holds insulation, wiring or plumbing that must be disturbed
  • Wall finish: smooth, textured, tile, or plaster
  • Test results on older materials, and any abatement that follows
  • How much paint is needed to blend the repair into the room

Get several written bids if you can. The CSLB suggests collecting three bids and capping any deposit at the lesser of 10 percent or $1,000. Once labor and materials reach $1,000, the work belongs to a licensed contractor. For help sizing up your own wall, our water damage repair page lays out the process.

Local note

Calabasas homes and the 1978 dividing line

The January 2021 Housing Element Background Report from the City of Calabasas puts 73 percent of Calabasas housing stock was built before 1989, and the 1980s were the largest building decade at 37 percent. It describes the older neighborhoods as mostly dating to the 1960s, 1970s and 1980s. The city counts 9,230 housing units as of 2020.

That puts a large share of local homes near the 1945 to 1978 window for asbestos and the pre-1978 line for lead paint. Fewer than two hundred buildings in the city predate 1960, so wet walls here are mostly gypsum board and plaster is the exception. Even so, remodels and additions from earlier decades mean you should not assume the wall in front of you matches the year on the deed. Test small samples before sanding or cutting.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a permit to repair a wall after water damage in Calabasas?

It depends on what the job touches. Replacing a patch of drywall is different from opening framing or moving electrical and plumbing. The city's Building and Safety Division is the office to ask, and it is better to ask before the work starts than after.

Your contractor should also know the answer for your scope. The same crews also work in neighboring Agoura Hills and Westlake Village, but permit questions there go to those cities.

How long until I can repaint after a wall gets wet?

Not until moisture readings match a dry reference and any joint compound has cured. Rushing the paint seals moisture in.

Your crew should give you the reading, not just a date.

Can a licensed handyman do the repair?

Check the license and the job size. The licensing threshold moved from $500 to $1,000 in labor and materials on January 1, 2025, and anything at or above it needs a CSLB license.

Confirm the license on the CSLB website and get the contract in writing.

Will insurance cover the wall repair?

Sudden and accidental damage, such as a pipe that burst, is often covered, and repair is part of a typical claim. Slow leaks and neglect are commonly excluded, and so is flood. Your policy and your insurer hold the answer.

CDI also recommends temporary repairs and saved receipts.

What if the test shows asbestos?

Stop the work in that area. Asbestos abatement is done by registered abatement contractors, not by a general crew or by you.

Your restoration company can dry the rest of the structure while the abatement is arranged. See our water damage cleanup page for how removal is handled.
