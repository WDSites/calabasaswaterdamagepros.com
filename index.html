Steps to take while you wait for the crew

  1. Stop the source. Shut the main water valve, or the fixture valve if the leak is at a toilet, sink or washing machine.
  2. Cut power to wet rooms at the breaker, but only when the panel is reachable without standing in water.
  3. Lift valuables, rugs and electronics off wet floors and keep children and pets out of any water that may be contaminated.
  4. Take photos and short videos of the damage before anything is moved. Your insurer will ask for them.
  5. Call the 24/7 line and tell the dispatcher what failed, which rooms are wet and whether the water looks clean or dirty.

How the restoration process works

Every job follows the same logic even when the details differ. Water is removed, wet materials are sorted into dry-in-place or remove, the structure is dried to target readings, and only then does rebuilding begin. Skipping a step is how people end up with a freshly painted wall over wet studs.

  1. Call and triage

    The dispatcher asks what failed, how long water has been running, where it is, and whether it looks clean, gray or contaminated. That decides what the crew brings.

  2. Inspect and map the moisture

    Technicians use moisture meters and often a thermal camera to find water you cannot see behind baseboards, under cabinets and inside wall cavities.

  3. Extract standing water

    Truck-mounted or portable extractors pull water from hard floors and carpet. Removing liquid is much faster than evaporating it, so this comes before any drying equipment.

  4. Remove what cannot be saved

    Saturated carpet pad, wet insulation and drywall that has wicked water upward are cut out. Sewage-contaminated porous materials generally go.

  5. Dry with air movers and dehumidifiers

    Low-grain refrigerant or desiccant dehumidifiers and directed air movers run for days, with daily readings to confirm the structure is trending toward its dry standard.

  6. Sanitize, then rebuild

    Surfaces are cleaned and treated, the crew documents final readings, and repair work replaces what was removed.

Water damage and homeowners insurance

What a policy usually does and does not cover

Sudden and accidental damage, such as a burst pipe or an appliance failure, is often covered under a standard homeowners policy. Slow leaks and long-term neglect often are not. Flood, meaning water entering from outside, is usually excluded, and flood cover is a separate policy, commonly through the federal NFIP.

California policies expect you to take reasonable steps to protect the property after a loss, and the Department of Insurance says insurers may reimburse reasonable protection costs when you save receipts. Your policy wording and your insurer settle what applies to you. We document conditions with photos and moisture readings, but the coverage decision is between you and your carrier.

Mold after water damage

Mold needs moisture to grow. The EPA states that most mold will not grow if wet materials are dried within 24 to 48 hours, which is the main reason fast drying matters. Frequently asked questions

How fast do I need to call after finding water damage in my Calabasas home?

As soon as you find it. Drywall, carpet pad and insulation start absorbing water immediately. EPA guidance is to dry affected areas inside 24 to 48 hours, because after that mold becomes a real risk. Past that window, more materials shift from dry-in-place to remove.

If you cannot reach a plumber yet, shut the main valve and call the 24/7 line anyway. Extraction and drying can start while the pipe repair is arranged.

Do you work nights and weekends?

Yes. The emergency line is answered 24/7, including holidays and storm nights, and a crew is dispatched based on availability. We say same-day response when available, because we will not promise an arrival time we cannot control.

Will my homeowners insurance pay for the work?

That depends on your policy and the cause of the loss. Sudden and accidental damage is often covered, gradual leaks often are not, and flood usually needs separate flood coverage. Your policy and your insurer settle it, since we have no say in claims.

What we can do is document the loss with photos, readings and itemized scope notes so your claim has clear records.

Can I stay in the house while it dries?

Often yes, if the water was clean and the electrical system is safe. Expect loud air movers and dehumidifiers running around the clock for several days. If the water was sewage or floodwater, or if there is mold growth, your crew may ask you to stay out of the affected rooms or the whole home for a time.

What is the difference between water mitigation and water damage repair?

Mitigation is stopping the damage: extraction, removal of ruined materials and drying. Repair is rebuilding afterward, such as new drywall, paint and flooring. Mitigation comes first, and repair should not start until moisture readings show the structure is dry.

Our pages on water mitigation and water damage repair cover each in detail.

Do you give exact prices on the phone?

Before any work begins we go over scope and price, but the actual figure depends on the water category, the area affected and how long it has been wet. Call the 24/7 line and describe the situation. The dispatcher can tell you what will drive the cost.

Is a flooded garage or lower level handled the same way as a burst pipe?

Not quite. Outside water from storms or a creek is usually Category 2 or 3, which means more protective equipment and more removal of porous materials. Details are on the flood damage restoration page. Mention any basement or lower level when you call.

Who fixes the broken pipe?

A licensed plumber. Restoration crews dry and rebuild but do not repair the failed pipe or fixture. Many homeowners arrange both at once so water extraction can start while the plumber works.
