Water Heater Leaking in Calabasas, CA: What to Do First

A failing tank can put out dozens of gallons before anyone opens the closet door. This is the order that protects your safety first and your walls and floors second.

Call (818) 337-2642

By the Calabasas Water Damage Pros team · Updated October 28, 2026

Stop the fuel and the water first

Start with your nose. If you smell gas, often described as rotten eggs, do not touch a light switch, do not look for a valve and do not try to relight anything. Walk everyone outside, then call SoCalGas at 1-800-427-2200 from a safe distance, and dial 911 if anyone is in danger.

  1. Close the cold supply valve

    Find the shutoff on the cold pipe that feeds the top of the tank and turn the handle clockwise until it stops. With a lever, closed means the lever has swung across the line of the pipe.

  2. Cut the power or gas

    On an electric heater, flip its breaker off, and do that only when you can reach the panel on dry footing. On a gas heater, turn the control dial to Off or Pilot.

  3. Close the main valve if the tank keeps filling

    A seized cold valve or a split pipe means water keeps coming. Shut the main house valve, then open one faucet at the lowest point of the house to relieve pressure.

  4. Clear the floor

    Keep kids and pets off wet slab and tile. Lift cardboard boxes, paint cans and anything electrical off the floor before they wick water.

If water is pooling around an outlet, a power strip or the panel itself, leave the electricity alone and stay out of the room. That is a job for a licensed electrician or a crew that can isolate power safely.

Draining the tank without making it worse

A typical residential tank holds 40 to 50 gallons, and that water can be scalding for hours after the burner stops. If what comes out is too hot to touch, wait. Once it has cooled, screw a garden hose onto the drain valve near the bottom and run it to the driveway or a floor drain.

Open a hot tap on an upper floor so air can replace the water, then open the drain valve. Draining lowers the head of water pushing on a cracked tank, so the leak slows and the weight in the tank drops before a plumber arrives.

Draining does not tell you why it failed. Note a wet ring under the tank, rusty streaks near the base, or a drip from the relief valve pipe, and pass that to the plumber.

What the water actually does in a garage or closet

Heaters in Calabasas homes usually sit in a garage corner, a utility closet or a hallway alcove. Each setting traps water differently, and the puddle you can see is rarely the whole story.

Water from a tank or supply line starts out clean, which the industry calls Category 1. Left sitting for a day or two, it picks up dirt and bacteria from the materials it crosses and can slide toward Category 2. That clock is why the first few hours carry so much weight.

Plumber first, then drying

A leak from a fitting, a valve or the relief pipe is often a repair. Water seeping from the tank body itself usually means the tank is finished and needs replacing. A licensed plumber makes that call, and a restoration crew does not repair or replace heaters.

What the crew does is remove water and dry what it touched. If there is standing water in the garage or a flooded closet, pumping and extraction comes first, and wet-vac passes alone will not reach the drywall cavity. Once the plumber has the leak stopped, the crew can start drying without worrying the source is still running.

How a crew dries a heater closet

The goal is to find every wet inch, not only the visible ones. Here is the usual toolkit for a closet or garage corner.

  • A pin or pinless moisture meter, read at several heights up the wall to find where the wet line really ends.
  • A thermal camera, which shows cooler damp zones behind paint and inside the cavity.
  • Baseboard removal and small drywall openings near the floor, so air can reach the studs and bottom plate.
  • Air movers aimed along the wall and a low-grain-refrigerant dehumidifier to pull moisture out of the air.
  • Daily readings, so the end of drying is set by numbers rather than by a calendar.

That process falls under water mitigation, and it usually runs for several days. Rebuilding anything that had to come out, such as a section of drywall or baseboard, is separate repair work done once the readings are back to normal.

Insurance and a leaking heater

Check the wording on sudden versus gradual

A burst tank is the kind of sudden event many policies respond to, whereas a drip that ran unnoticed for months often falls outside them. Wording varies, so put the question to your insurer. California's Department of Insurance says policyholders should make temporary repairs to prevent further damage and keep receipts for protection costs. Deciding the claim is the insurer's job, not the drying crew's.

Frequently asked questions

How long can a water heater leak before it damages the wall?

There is no fixed number. A fast failure can soak a closet floor in minutes, while a slow drip may take weeks to show. Drywall paper softens and baseboard swells within a day or two of constant contact, so shut the supply off the moment you spot water.

Should I mop it up and wait to see if it stops?

No. A tank that has started leaking from its body does not repair itself. Mopping the surface leaves water behind the baseboard and under the heater. Close the cold valve first, then clear the floor, then call a plumber and a mitigation crew if the wall or flooring is wet.

Whose number do I dial in Calabasas when the water is coming from the utility pipe outside?

Phone (818) 251-2100 for the Las Virgenes Municipal Water District, staffed around the clock. The district deals with water and sewer emergencies upstream of your meter. If you are unsure which side the leak is on, closing your own main valve and checking whether the meter dial still moves is a quick way to find out.

Does my homeowners policy cover a leaking water heater?

It depends on your policy and on whether the failure was sudden or gradual. Many policies respond to a sudden burst but not to the heater itself or to long-term seepage. Read the water damage section, take photos before cleanup and ask your insurer before you discard anything.

Is the water from a heater leak safe to touch?

Not in the first hour, because it can be hot enough to scald. Once cooled, water from the tank is usually clean supply water, but it can pick up contaminants if it sits on soiled flooring. Keep electrics out of it and wear boots while you clear the area.
